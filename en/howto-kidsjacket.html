How to make a kids' jacket

A simple open-front jacket. No collar; it fastens with buttons or snaps. There is no lining — the seam allowances are bound with bias tape. In a heavier cotton or corduroy it becomes the spring and autumn layer.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (one jacket, 120 cm height)

  • Main fabric (corduroy, Oxford cloth or canvas) — about 110 cm wide × 100 cm (43 × 39 in)
  • Bias binding, 12.7 mm folded — about 2.5 m
  • Buttons, 15 mm — 3 or 4
  • Sewing thread

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Lay the pattern on the wrong side and trace both lines. The back's center line is the fold.
  2. Cut the pieces
    Back on the fold, 2 fronts, 2 sleeves. With corduroy, keep the nap running the same way on every piece.
  3. Transfer the notches
    Mark the armhole on the body and the center of the sleeve head. Miss these and the sleeve twists.
  4. Finish the edges
    Zigzag the seam allowances.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the shoulders

  1. Place front and back right sides together and sew both shoulders.
  2. Press the allowances towards the back.

4-2. Set the sleeves

  1. Match the center notch of the sleeve head to the shoulder seam.
  2. The sleeve head is slightly longer than the armhole. Ease it in with a gathering row.
  3. Sew right sides together, round the whole armhole.

4-3. Sew the sides and underarms

  1. Place front and back right sides together and sew from the cuff to the hem in one run.
  2. Backstitch over the underarm junction.

4-4. Bind the edges

  1. Bind the neckline, the front edges, the hem and the cuffs with bias tape.
  2. Running the front edge in one continuous length gives the cleanest finish.
  3. Work the buttonholes and sew on the buttons.

✏️ The sleeve head is deliberately longer than the armhole — that extra is the room to move the arm. Ease it in with a gathering row rather than stretching the armhole to match. Forcing it puts creases at the underarm.

5. Tips & variations

  • Snaps instead of buttons: easier for small hands to manage alone.
  • Neat without a lining: bound allowances mean the inside looks finished too.
  • Corduroy nap: cut every piece the same way up, or the panels read as different colors.
