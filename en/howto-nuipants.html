How to make plush pants

Front and back from the same pattern piece: join the crotch curves and sew the inseam. Change the length and they become shorts or full-length trousers. Elastic is not worth it at this size — the waist is simply turned twice.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (one outfit for a 20 cm plush)

  • Main fabric (light cotton or lightweight denim) — about 20 × 15 cm
  • Size 9 needle and fine thread

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Measure the plush
    Chest at its widest, and the length you want from the shoulder. Two plushies both sold as "20 cm" can be quite different, so measure yours.
  2. Trace the pattern
    Lay the pattern on the wrong side and trace both lines. At this scale a fine chalk pencil works better than a marker.
  3. Cut the pieces
    If the fabric frays, seal the edges or zigzag them straight away.
  4. Transfer the notches
    The smaller the garment, the more the notches matter. Do not skip them.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the crotch curves

  1. Place the two fronts right sides together and sew the crotch curve.
  2. Do the same with the backs.
  3. Clip the curved allowance every 5 mm.

4-2. Sew the sides and inseam

  1. Place front and back right sides together and sew both sides.
  2. Then sew the inseam in one run, hem to hem.
  3. Backstitch over the crotch junction — it takes the pull every time they go on.

4-3. Finish the waist and hems

  1. Turn the waist under 5 mm, then again by the casing depth, and stitch.
  2. Turn the hems under 5 mm and stitch.
  3. If they will not go on, open the center back by about 1 cm and add hook-and-loop.

✏️ Backstitch over the crotch junction. The legs get pulled every time the plush is changed, and that point takes all of it. Two or three passes with a short stitch length and it will not give.

5. Tips & variations

  • Lightweight denim only: heavy denim cannot be sewn this small. Eight ounces or less.
  • Length changes everything: a short inseam gives shorts.
  • Turn-ups: cut them long and roll the hems.
  • Seam allowance is 5 mm: at 1 cm the allowances collide and the garment will not turn out cleanly.
  • Size 9 needle, fine thread: a heavy needle leaves holes in the fabric.
  • Mock it up in paper: assembling it once in interfacing or copy paper shows up any problem with length or ease.
