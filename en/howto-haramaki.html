How to make a belly warmer

A tube to keep the middle warm. Join a piece of jersey into a ring and turn the edges back — that is all of it. Cut it with the stretch running around the body so it does not squeeze. Under pajamas, or against air conditioning.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (one warmer, size M)

  • Jersey (interlock, rib or wool blend) — about 90 cm wide × 40 cm
  • A ballpoint needle (75/11) and stretch thread

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

2. Make the pattern (auto-generated in Katagami)

  2. Enter the size

  3. Set the seam allowance

  4. Print
    Press “Print (actual size)”, then in the print dialog set Scale = 100% and turn “Fit to page” OFF. Check the 50 mm calibration box on the first guide sheet with a ruler.

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Lay the pattern on the wrong side and trace the cutting line. Run the stretchiest direction across the width.
  2. Cut the piece
    Cut one.
  3. Check the stretch
    Pull the fabric both ways and confirm which stretches more before you place the pattern.

4. Sewing

4-1. Join into a ring

  1. Fold right sides together and sew the short edges into a ring.
  2. Use a ballpoint needle and a stretch stitch.
  3. Rather than pressing the allowance open, press it to one side and topstitch — it sits softer against the skin.

4-2. Turn the edges back

  1. Fold the top edge inwards by the turn-back amount and stitch all round.
  2. Do the same at the bottom.
  3. Use a stretch stitch, or the thread snaps when it is pulled on.

4-3. Finish

  1. Turn it inside out and check nothing rubs.
  2. Press on a low setting through a cloth, lightly.

✏️ The ease is negative — smaller than the actual measurement. Jersey stretches, so made to the exact measurement it slides down. About 6 cm under the waist measurement stays put without squeezing.

5. Tips & variations

  • Stretch runs across: cut the other way and it will not go on. Check before cutting.
  • Go longer: covering the lower back as well as the stomach changes how warm it feels.
  • Wool for winter: a wool blend for cold weather, cotton interlock for air conditioning.
