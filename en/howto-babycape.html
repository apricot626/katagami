How to make a baby cape

A cape you throw over a baby in a carrier or a pram. No sleeves, so it goes on easily and does not bunch up in a car seat. The pattern is a quarter-circle: fold the fabric in four, cut once, and the whole thing is done.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (size 0–1 year)

  • Main fabric (fleece, wool or double gauze) — about 110 cm wide × 90 cm (44 × 35 in)
  • Snap fasteners or covered buttons — 1–2 sets
  • Optional: fabric for a hood, or bias tape for the neckline

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Fold the fabric in four
    Fold it in half, then in half again. The corner where the two folds meet is the center of the cape.
  2. Place the pattern
    Line the right-angled corner of the quarter-circle pattern up with that folded corner and trace the neck arc and the hem arc.
  3. Cut
    Cut through all four layers. Opened out you have a doughnut. Cut one straight line from the outer edge to the neck — that is the front opening.

4. Sewing

4-1. Finish the front edges and hem

  1. Turn both front edges under twice and stitch.
  2. Hem the outer arc the same way. It is a curve, so the outer edge is longer — ease it in gradually or press as you go.
  3. Fleece does not fray, so you can leave the hem raw.

4-2. Finish the neckline

  1. Sew bias tape around the inner arc, right sides together, then turn it to the inside and stitch it down.
  2. Without tape, clip the allowance every 7–8 mm before folding — a curve that is not clipped will not lie flat.

4-3. Add the fastening

  1. Fit one or two snaps near the neck at the front. Two, one above the other, keeps the wind out.
  2. If you use buttons, sew them on with doubled thread — a baby will pull at them.

✏️ A circular piece stretches under its own weight, most of all on the bias. Finish the neckline the same day you cut it, and store it flat rather than on a hanger.

5. Tips & variations

  • Add a hood: attach the hood from the hoodie pattern to the neckline and it becomes far warmer.
  • Make it reversible: cut two, sew right sides together and turn. You skip the front and hem folds, so it is barely more work.
  • Fleece is fastest: it does not fray, so only the neckline needs finishing. A good first project.
