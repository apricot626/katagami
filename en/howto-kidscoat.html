How to make a kids' coat

A simple unlined coat in child sizes. Two fronts, one back, two sleeves and a stand collar — the same construction as a shirt, in heavier fabric. Wool or quilted cotton keeps it warm without needing a lining.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (size 110 cm)

  • Main fabric (wool, quilted cotton or heavy twill) — about 110 cm wide × 130 cm (44 × 51 in)
  • Fusible interfacing — for the collar and front bands
  • Buttons — 4–5
  • Optional: lining fabric

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the back (on the fold), the fronts, the sleeves and the collar.
  2. Cut
    Cut one back, two fronts, two sleeves and two collar pieces, then fuse interfacing to one collar piece and the front bands.
  3. Finish the edges
    Serge or zigzag the shoulder, side and underarm allowances — thick fabric frays badly.

4. Sewing

4-1. Finish the front edges

  1. Turn each front edge under twice and stitch, pressing hard first so both sides match.

4-2. Shoulders and sleeves

  1. Sew both shoulder seams and press the allowances open.
  2. Match the center of each sleeve head to the shoulder seam and sew the sleeve in while everything is flat.

4-3. Make and attach the collar

  1. Sew the collar pieces right sides together, leaving the lower edge open. Trim the corners, turn and press.
  2. Sandwich the neckline between the collar layers and sew all the way around, then clip the neckline curve.

4-4. Sides, hem and buttons

  1. Sew from the cuff along the underarm and down to the hem in one line.
  2. Turn the cuffs and hem under twice and stitch. In thick fabric, slipstitching by hand handles the bulk better.
  3. Work buttonholes and sew on the buttons — large ones are easier for small hands.

✏️ Thick fabric needs a bigger needle. A size 14 or 16 will get through wool and quilted cotton; a universal 11 will snap where the seams cross.

5. Tips & variations

  • Big buttons: 2 cm (¾ in) or more, so a child can do the coat up without help.
  • Add a lining: cut the same pattern in lining and it slides on over a jumper instead of sticking.
  • Add a hood: borrow the hood from the kids' hoodie and attach it to the neckline instead of a collar.
