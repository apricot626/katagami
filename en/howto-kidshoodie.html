How to make a kids' hoodie

A pullover hoodie in child sizes. Four pattern pieces, and the hood is just two pieces sewn together and joined to the neckline. In French terry or sweatshirt fleece it becomes the thing they reach for every day.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (size 110 cm)

  • Main fabric (French terry or sweatshirt fleece) — about 150 cm wide × 110 cm (59 × 43 in)
  • Ballpoint machine needle and stretch thread
  • Optional: ribbing for the cuffs and hem

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

2. Make the pattern (auto-generated in Katagami)

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the front, back, sleeves and hood.
  2. Cut
    Cut one front and one back on the fold, two sleeves and two hood pieces.
  3. Finish the edges
    Optional on knit.

4. Sewing

4-1. Shoulders and sleeves

  1. Sew both shoulder seams with a stretch stitch.
  2. Match the center of each sleeve head to the shoulder seam and sew the sleeves in.

4-2. Sides and underarms

  1. Sew from the cuff along the underarm and down to the hem in one continuous line.

4-3. Make and attach the hood

  1. Sew the two hood pieces right sides together along the curved back edge.
  2. Turn the front edge of the hood under twice and stitch.
  3. Pin the lower edge of the hood to the neckline and sew all the way around.

4-4. Cuffs and hem

  1. Turn the cuffs and hem under and finish with a stretch stitch, or attach ribbing stretched slightly as you sew.

✏️ Children put clothes under real strain. Backstitch over the underarm and side junctions — those two points are where a home-made garment gives way first.

5. Tips & variations

  • Size up: children grow fast. Making the next size and turning back the cuffs buys you a year.
  • No drawcord: hood cords are a strangulation hazard on playground equipment. Leave them off for small children.
  • Add a name tag: stitched inside the hem, it saves the lost property box.
