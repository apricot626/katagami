How to make a kids' apron

Over the head, with the waist ties knotted at the back. Make the neck loop from elastic and they can put it on alone. For cooking lessons and for helping out. Made to match a triangular headscarf, it becomes a proper set.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (one apron, 120–130 cm height)

  • Main fabric (Oxford cloth, sheeting or linen) — about 110 cm wide × 70 cm (43 × 28 in)
  • Flat elastic, 1.5 cm — about 45 cm for the neck loop
  • Sewing thread

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Lay the pattern on the wrong side and trace both lines. The center line is the fold.
  2. Cut the pieces
    The body on the fold, one or two pockets, two waist ties.
  3. Transfer the notches
    Mark the top of the bib, where the side curve begins.

4. Sewing

4-1. Finish the edges

  1. Turn the diagonal side edges under twice, 1 cm each time, and stitch.
  2. Do the same on both sides of the bib.
  3. Diagonal edges stretch easily. Press the folds firmly before sewing.

4-2. Add the pocket

  1. Turn the pocket's top edge under twice and stitch.
  2. Press the other three allowances to the wrong side.
  3. Position it and stitch round three sides.
  4. Triangles of backstitching at the top corners stop it pulling off.

4-3. Attach the neck loop

  1. Turn the top of the bib under twice, catching the ends of the elastic as you go.
  2. Decide the elastic length against the actual child — long enough for the head, short enough not to sag.
  3. Backstitch two or three times over the ends.

4-4. Ties and hem

  1. Fold each waist tie in four and topstitch.
  2. Catch a tie at each side notch and backstitch it in place.
  3. Hem the bottom.

✏️ An elastic neck loop lets a child put the apron on unaided. Set the length against the actual child: too short and it will not go over the head, too long and the bib hangs uselessly low. Pin it, have them try it on, and only then stitch it down.

5. Tips & variations

  • Match the headscarf: the triangular headscarf in the same fabric makes the cooking-lesson set.
  • Somewhere for a name: a name tape behind the pocket or on a tie end saves it going astray.
  • Divide the pocket: a line of stitching down the middle makes two.
