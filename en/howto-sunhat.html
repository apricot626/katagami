How to make a bucket hat

A classic bucket hat in three pieces: the flat crown, the side band and the brim. Everything joins in a circle, which is the only skill involved — and it is the same skill three times over. Cotton or linen, ideally with interfacing in the brim.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (adult size)

  • Main fabric (cotton, linen or canvas) — about 60 × 60 cm (24 × 24 in)
  • Lining fabric — the same amount
  • Fusible interfacing — for the brim
  • Optional: a cord and eyelets for a chin strap

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the crown (a circle), the side band and the brim (a ring).
  2. Cut
    Cut each piece in both main and lining fabric. Fuse interfacing to the brim pieces.
  3. Finish the edges
    Not needed if you are lining it.

4. Sewing

4-1. Make the brim

  1. Sew the two brim rings right sides together along the outer edge.
  2. Clip the curve, turn and press. Topstitch concentric rings across the brim — that is what makes it hold its shape.

4-2. Make the crown and band

  1. Sew the side band into a loop, then sew the crown circle to its upper edge.
  2. Pin at four points first, then fill in between. Starting at one end guarantees you run short at the other.
  3. Assemble the lining the same way.

4-3. Assemble

  1. Pin the brim to the lower edge of the outer band, right sides together, and sew.
  2. Slip the lining over it, right sides together, and sew around the same edge, leaving a gap.
  3. Turn through the gap, close it, push the lining inside and topstitch around the base of the crown.

✏️ Every seam here joins two circles. Pin at four points, then halve the gaps, then halve again. Sew from one end and the last few centimeters will never match.

5. Tips & variations

  • Interface the brim: without it the brim flops and the hat looks unfinished.
  • Reversible: use two good fabrics and it works either way out.
  • Add a chin cord: two eyelets and a cord keeps it on in wind, which matters for children.
