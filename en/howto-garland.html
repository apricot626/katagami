How to make a flag garland (bunting)

A party and event classic: a row of triangle flags. Sew the flags, then sandwich their tops in store-bought bias tape or ribbon and sew. Change the fabric and it works all year — birthdays, Christmas, Halloween, Easter. Great for using up scraps.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (For the standard size (10 flags))

  • Main fabric (cotton or felt) — about 110 × 55 cm (for 10 flags; double if lined)
  • Store-bought bias tape or ribbon (for the tops) — enough for the full length
  • Optional: fusible interfacing (to stiffen the flags)
  • Sewing thread

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Decide the length
    From the space you want to fill, decide how many flags you need — roughly the flag top width plus 2 cm of spacing each.
  2. Cut
    Cut one flag per pennant (two per flag if lined). Felt can stay single-layer. For the tape, allow the full length plus about 20 cm of tie at each end.

4. Sewing

4-1. Make the flags

  1. If lining, place two layers right sides together, sew the two lower edges (leave the top open), clip the point and turn out.
  2. In felt, use a single layer with raw edges. Trimming the edges with pinking shears keeps them from fraying and looks decorative.

4-2. Sandwich in the tape

  1. Open the bias tape and tuck the flag tops in at even spacing, pinning as you go.
  2. Sew the tape closed along its whole length, catching each flag. Leave a tie at each end to hook or pin it up.

✏️ Flag size and count are up to you: many small flags look delicate, a few big ones look bold. Instead of tape you can sew the flags onto yarn or jute twine for a rustic look.

5. Tips & variations

  • Change colors by event: bright for birthdays, orange and black for Halloween, red and green for Christmas, pastels for Easter.
  • Add letters: applique felt letters onto the flags for a "HAPPY BIRTHDAY" banner.
  • Change the shape: square or rounded bottoms work as well as triangles.
