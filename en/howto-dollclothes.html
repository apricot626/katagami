How to make a doll dress

An A-line dress for a doll. One front, two backs, snaps down the spine. Measure your own doll's chest and length and it will fit whatever you have. The pieces are tiny, which also makes it a low-stakes way to practice techniques you would not risk on a full-size garment.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (27 cm doll)

  • Main fabric (lawn, broadcloth or double gauze — something thin) — about 40 × 30 cm (16 × 12 in)
  • Tiny snap fasteners or narrow hook-and-loop — 2–3 sets
  • Narrow bias tape (about 6 mm / ¼ in finished) — about 60 cm (24 in)
  • Optional: narrow lace or ribbon

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

2. Make the pattern (auto-generated in Katagami)

  2. Enter the size

  3. Set the seam allowance
    Use the slider at the bottom (beginners: 1.0–1.5 cm / about 3/8–5/8 in).
  4. Print
    Press “Print (actual size)”, then in the print dialog set Scale = 100% and turn “Fit to page” OFF. Check the 50 mm calibration box on the first guide sheet with a ruler.

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Use a mechanical pencil rather than chalk — a thick chalk line is a 1–2 mm error at this scale.
  2. Cut
    Cut one front on the fold and two backs.
  3. Finish the edges
    Seam allowances are 5 mm, so pinking shears are more manageable than a zigzag.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the shoulders

  1. Place front and backs right sides together and sew both shoulder seams with a 5 mm allowance.
  2. Press the allowances to one side rather than open — at this scale, pressing open adds visible bulk.

4-2. Bind the neckline and armholes

  1. Sew narrow bias tape around the neckline, turn it to the inside and topstitch.
  2. Bind the armholes the same way.
  3. Wide tape will not follow a doll-sized neckline. Use narrow tape, or cut your own bias strips from the main fabric.

4-3. Sew the sides

  1. Sew both side seams from the underarm to the hem, backstitching at the underarm.

4-4. Finish the back and hem

  1. Turn each back opening edge under twice — 5 mm and 5 mm — and stitch.
  2. Hem the lower edge the same way, easing the flare in as you fold.
  3. Fit two or three snaps, or narrow hook-and-loop, down the back.

✏️ Two things decide whether doll clothes look right: a 5 mm seam allowance and thin fabric. A standard 1 cm allowance makes the side seams so bulky the dress will not go on.

5. Tips & variations

  • Hook-and-loop is easier: much simpler for a child to manage than tiny snaps.
  • Small prints only: a large print puts no complete motif on a garment this size.
  • Practice piece: bias-bound necklines and flared hems, in miniature and using almost no fabric.
