Sewing knit fabric

You do not need a serger to sew knits. What you do need is the right needle, a stitch that stretches and a way to stop the edge going wavy. Get those three right and an ordinary machine handles jersey perfectly well.

1. Change the needle and thread

  • Needle: a ballpoint (jersey) needle. A sharp needle cuts the loops of the knit and leaves a ladder of holes along the seam.
  • Thread: a stretch thread (or ordinary polyester, which has some give). Cotton thread snaps.
  • Size: 75/11 for light jersey, 90/14 for sweatshirting.

2. Use a stitch that stretches

A straight stitch has no give, so it snaps the first time the seam is pulled. Use one of these instead.

  • Narrow zigzag (width 0.5–1 mm, length 2.5 mm) — the simplest answer, available on any machine.
  • Stretch stitch (the lightning-bolt symbol) if your machine has one.
  • Twin needle for hems — two lines on the front, a zigzag underneath, which is what shop-bought T-shirts use.

3. Stop the wavy edge

A seam that comes out rippled means the fabric was stretched while it fed through.

  • Do not pull the fabric. Let the feed dogs take it and only guide it.
  • Lower the presser foot pressure if your machine allows it.
  • Use a walking foot — it feeds both layers at the same rate.
  • Stabilize the shoulders with clear elastic or a strip of interfacing in the seam. Shoulders carry the weight of the garment and stretch out permanently otherwise.

4. Hems and cuffs

  1. Fold the hem up once (there is no need to double-fold — knit does not fray) and press.
  2. Stitch with a twin needle or a narrow zigzag. Stitching from the right side keeps the twin-needle lines even.
  3. For a neckband, cut the ribbing to about 0.8–0.85× the neckline measurement, join it into a loop, and stretch it slightly as you sew it on. That is what stops the neckline gaping.

Frequently asked questions

The seam breaks when I pull it.
You used a straight stitch. Use a narrow zigzag or a stretch stitch — a straight stitch has no give at all.
The fabric gets pushed down into the needle plate.
That happens with fine jersey. Lay tissue paper under it, sew through both and tear the paper away, or use a straight-stitch needle plate.
Do I need a serger?
No. A serger is faster and neater on the raw edges, but a home machine with a ballpoint needle and a zigzag makes a perfectly sound garment.
