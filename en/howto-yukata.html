How to make a yukata

Traditional Japanese summer wear cut entirely from straight lines. Four kinds of rectangle — body panels, sleeves, okumi (front panels) and collar — and not a single curve, so it is approachable even if you have never sewn a kimono. Cotton or cotton-linen makes a casual, wearable version.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (women's size)

  • Main fabric (cotton, cotton-linen or shibori; a traditional bolt is about 36 cm / 14 in wide) — about 11 m (12 yd)
  • Hand sewing thread (kimono is traditionally hand sewn, but a machine works)
  • Optional: collar stiffener

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Check the measurements
    Every piece is a rectangle, so skip printing and mark the measurements from the guide sheet straight onto the fabric — a yukata is long and would take a great many sheets.
  2. Cut
    Cut two body panels (folded at the shoulder), two sleeves, two okumi and one collar.
  3. Finish the edges
    Traditional kimono seam allowances are folded rather than serged, so no edge finishing is needed.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the body

  1. Fold each body panel at the shoulder and sew the two center back edges together.
  2. Sew the side seams, leaving the sleeve opening (the miyatsuguchi) unsewn.

4-2. Make and attach the sleeves

  1. Fold each sleeve at the top and sew the underarm seam, leaving the cuff opening unsewn.
  2. Turn the cuff and the rounded lower sleeve edge under twice and stitch.
  3. Pin each sleeve to its opening on the body, right sides together, and sew.

4-3. Attach the okumi

  1. Sew an okumi panel to each front edge, right sides together.
  2. Press the seam allowances towards the body.

4-4. Attach the collar

  1. Fold the collar in half lengthwise and press.
  2. Sew one edge of the collar to the garment in one continuous run, from the end of one okumi, around the back neck, to the end of the other.
  3. Turn the collar out, fold the remaining edge under and slipstitch it down.

4-5. Hem

  1. Turn the hem under twice and slipstitch.

✏️ In Japanese garment sewing you press seam allowances to one side rather than open, and you keep every allowance falling the same way. Do that and no seam shows from the outside — the principle holds whether you sew by hand or by machine.

5. Tips & variations

  • Start child-sized: far less fabric and far less sewing. Make one in the child size before committing 11 m of fabric.
  • A machine is fine: it is all straight seams. Slipstitch only the collar by hand and it still looks right.
  • Make a set: a jinbei in the same fabric gives the whole family matching summer wear.
