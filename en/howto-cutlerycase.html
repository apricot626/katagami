How to make a cutlery roll

A roll that holds chopsticks, a spoon and a fork in separate slots, then ties shut. Lay a pocket panel over the body and stitch vertical dividers — that is the whole construction. Change the count and the slot width and the same pattern holds paintbrushes, knitting needles or colored pencils.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (one roll, 4-piece set)

  • Outer fabric (linen, canvas or oxford cloth) — about 30 cm × 40 cm
  • Lining fabric — the same amount
  • Fabric for the pocket — about 25 cm × 15 cm
  • Matching fabric or cotton tape, 1.5 cm wide, for the tie — about 60 cm

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Measure what goes in
    Measure the longest piece of cutlery and the widest one. Chopsticks set the length; a spoon sets the width.
  2. Trace the pattern
    Trace the body, the pocket panel and the tie. The notches along the pocket panel mark the dividing lines.
  3. Cut the pieces
    Cut 1 body in the outer, 1 in the lining, 1 pocket panel and 1 tie.
  4. Finish the edges
    Not needed — it is turned right side out.

4. Sewing

4-1. Hem the top of the pocket panel

  1. Double-fold the top edge of the pocket panel by 1 cm and 1 cm again and stitch. That is the opening the cutlery slides into.
  2. Do it first. Once the panel is on the body you cannot reach this edge.

4-2. Lay the pocket on the body

  1. Spread the outer body right side up and lay the pocket panel on it, bottom edges aligned.
  2. Baste the sides and the bottom, inside the seam allowance.
  3. Do not sew the top edge. If it is closed, nothing goes in.

4-3. Stitch the dividers

  1. Stitch from the top of the pocket down to the bottom at each notch. That creates one slot per piece.
  2. Always backstitch at the start of each line, at the top of the pocket. That is where a roll tears with use.
  3. Spoons and forks are different widths. Nudging the divider positions to match your actual cutlery gets a snug fit.

4-4. Assemble and add the tie

  1. Fold the tie in four and topstitch.
  2. Baste the end of the tie to the outer body, halfway up one side edge — the point it comes round to when the roll is closed.
  3. Place the outer and lining right sides together and sew all the way round, leaving an 8 cm opening.
  4. Trim the corners diagonally, turn right side out through the opening and press.
  5. Topstitch 3 mm from the edge all the way round, which closes the opening too.

✏️ The pocket is a little over half the length of the cutlery. Deeper and it is awkward to get things out; shallower and they work loose when you roll it. Rolled up, the flap should fold over the tips — that is how you know the proportions are right.

5. Tips & variations

  • For packed lunches: rolled and tied, nothing rattles loose in your bag. It is the thing that finally gets you off disposables.
  • Water-resistant lining: cutlery goes back in damp, so a coated lining saves the roll.
  • Not just cutlery: 8 slots at 3.5 cm each turns it into a brush or knitting needle roll. Same pattern.
