How to make a bag-in-bag organizer

An insert that stops everything in a large tote settling at the bottom. Stitch the divider vertically and pens, phone and keys each get a place. Change bags and you lift the whole thing across. It needs firm fabric or it collapses and defeats the point.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (standard size)

  • Main fabric (canvas, oxford or quilted cotton) — about 110 cm wide × 60 cm (43 × 24 in)
  • Divider fabric — about 50 × 40 cm (20 × 16 in)
  • Heavy fusible interfacing — for the body
  • Optional: webbing for the handles

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Measure the outer bag
    Measure the inside of the bag it goes into and make this 1–2 cm (½ in) smaller in width and height.
  2. Trace the pattern
    Trace the body (base on the fold), the dividers and the handles.
  3. Cut
    Cut one body, one or two dividers and two handles, then fuse heavy interfacing to the body.

4. Sewing

4-1. Make the dividers

  1. Turn the top edge of each divider under twice and stitch. Do this first.
  2. With two dividers, make them different heights so the contents are visible.

4-2. Attach the dividers

  1. Lay the body flat and position a divider the divider-height above the base fold.
  2. Stitch vertically to create the pockets — about 2 cm (¾ in) for a pen, about 8 cm (3 in) for a phone.
  3. Backstitch at the top of each line; that is where pockets tear.
  4. Baste the lower and side edges within the seam allowance.
  5. All of this must happen before the body becomes a tube — the machine arm cannot reach inside afterwards.

4-3. Sew the sides and gusset

  1. Fold the body at the base, right sides together, and sew both sides.
  2. Pinch each base corner into a triangle and sew across at the gusset depth. Check both sides match before sewing.
  3. Trim the triangles to 1 cm and finish the edges.

4-4. Finish the opening and handles

  1. Turn the opening edge under twice and stitch.
  2. Stitch the handles inside the opening with a rectangle and a cross. They only have to lift the organizer out, so they can be short.

✏️ Everything involving the dividers happens while the body is flat. Once the sides are sewn it becomes a tube, and there is no way to stitch a divider inside a tube on a domestic machine.

5. Tips & variations

  • Measure the host bag: 1–2 cm smaller in each direction is what makes it easy to lift in and out.
  • Dividers on both faces: front and back doubles the organization.
  • Firm interfacing matters: a soft organizer slumps inside the bag and does nothing at all.
