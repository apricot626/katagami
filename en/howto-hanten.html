How to make a hanten (japanese padded jacket)

The Japanese padded jacket you live in all winter. Not one curve anywhere — the body panels fold at the shoulder, so there is not even a shoulder seam to sew. Add a layer of batting and it becomes the genuinely warm article rather than a light robe.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (women's size)

  • Main fabric (Japanese-print cotton, chirimen or flannel) — about 110 cm wide × 300 cm (43 × 118 in)
  • Lining fabric (lightweight cotton) — the same amount
  • Medium batting — about 200 cm (79 in) if you are padding it
  • Optional: ties or a decorative cord for the front

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Check the measurements
    Body, sleeves and collar are all rectangles, so mark them straight onto the fabric from the guide sheet rather than printing.
  2. Cut
    Cut two body panels (folded at the shoulder), two sleeves and one collar.
  3. Open the center front
    Cut the front half of each body panel open down the center — that is where you get into it.
  4. If padding
    Cut the batting to the same dimensions as the body and sleeves.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the center back

  1. Place the two body panels right sides together and sew the center back seam. The shoulders are folds, so there is nothing to sew there.
  2. If you are padding it, layer outer, batting and lining now and quilt a grid across them to lock the layers together.

4-2. Make and attach the sleeves

  1. Fold each sleeve in half and sew the underarm seam, leaving the cuff open.
  2. Sew the open edge of each sleeve to the body between the notches. Both edges are straight, so if the notches match, the seam matches.

4-3. Sew the sides

  1. Sew each side from below the sleeve opening down to the hem, reinforcing where the sleeve meets the body.

4-4. Attach the collar

  1. Fold the collar in half lengthwise and sew one edge to the garment in one run: front edge, around the back neck, down to the other front edge.
  2. Turn it out, fold the remaining edge under and slipstitch or topstitch it down.
  3. The collar goes on as one straight line — there is no curve to ease it around.

4-5. Cuffs and hem

  1. Turn the cuffs and hem under twice and stitch. If it is padded, hand slipstitching handles the bulk better than a machine.
  2. Add ties at the front if you want it to fasten.

✏️ If you are padding it, do not skip the quilting grid. Batting that is only caught at the edges shifts to one side in the first wash and never comes back. A grid every 10–15 cm (4–6 in) fixes it permanently.

5. Tips & variations

  • Do not print the pattern: it is all rectangles. Ruling the lines straight onto the fabric is faster and more accurate.
  • Skip the padding: in flannel or fleece as a single layer it becomes a light spring jacket.
  • Child size: the child preset makes the version worn for festivals and shichi-go-san.
