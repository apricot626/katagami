How to make a school satchel cover

A cover for the flap of a randoseru, the Japanese school satchel. It keeps the leather dry and unscratched, and a strip of reflective tape makes a child far more visible on a dark walk home. Water-repellent or laminated fabric is the point of it.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (standard size)

  • Main fabric (water-repellent fabric, laminate or nylon) — about 40 × 45 cm (16 × 18 in)
  • Wide bias tape — about 120 cm (47 in)
  • Reflective tape — about 25 cm (10 in)
  • Narrow flat elastic (0.6 cm / ¼ in) — about 40 cm (16 in)
  • Optional: clear vinyl (about 0.3 mm)

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Measure the satchel
    Measure the flap width and its length from the hinge to the tip, and note the radius of the rounded end.
  2. Trace the pattern
    Trace the body. The marks show suggested elastic positions.
  3. Cut
    Cut one body. Use clips rather than pins — laminate keeps every hole.
  4. Finish the edges
    Not needed; the edges are bound.

4. Sewing

4-1. Attach the reflective tape

  1. Lay the tape along the curve at the lower end, on the right side, and stitch both long edges.
  2. Change direction gradually to follow the curve.
  3. Do this first — once the edge is bound, the tape ends have nowhere to hide.

4-2. Bind the edges

  1. Bind all the way around with wide bias tape, which follows the curve naturally.
  2. If you double-fold instead, ease the outer edge in around the curve.
  3. Laminate sticks to the presser foot: use a Teflon foot, or lay tissue paper on top and tear it away.

4-3. Attach the elastic

  1. Sew elastic loops at the center of the lower edge and at the marked points on each side.
  2. The lower loop hooks over the satchel's clasp; the side loops go over the strap fittings.
  3. Fit the cover to the satchel and check the positions before sewing them permanently. Too loose and it falls off; too tight and the flap will not close.

4-4. Finish the top edge

  1. Either add two self-fabric loops that hook over the satchel hardware, or leave the top edge to tuck under the fitting.
  2. For a version that holds a road-safety card, make the top edge into a pocket the card slides into.

✏️ Laminate and coated fabric keep every needle hole. You cannot unpick and try again, so fit the cover to the actual satchel and mark the elastic positions before you sew anything down. Clips, not pins.

5. Tips & variations

  • Reflective tape earns its place: it costs very little and transforms visibility on a dark walk. Do not leave it off.
  • Water-repellent fabric only: the whole purpose is rain, and ordinary cotton simply soaks through.
  • Make two: it gets splashed with mud constantly.
