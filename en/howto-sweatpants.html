How to make a sweatpants (joggers)

Sweatpants gathered into ribbing at the ankle. Front and back use the same pattern piece, so the sewing is three seams plus the waist and cuffs. French terry or sweatshirt fleece — the same fabric as the hoodie, if you want a set.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (men's M)

  • Main fabric (French terry or sweatshirt fleece) — about 150 cm wide × 160 cm (59 × 63 in)
  • Ribbing for the cuffs — about 40 cm (16 in)
  • Flat elastic (3 cm / 1¼ in wide) — your waist measurement
  • Optional: cord for the waist and eyelets

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the leg pattern and the cuff ribbing.
  2. Cut
    Cut two fronts and two backs from the same pattern, flipping it for the second of each pair, plus two cuffs.
  3. Finish the edges
    Serge or zigzag the allowances, or skip it — knit does not fray.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the crotch seams

  1. Sew the two fronts together along the center front curve, then the two backs.

4-2. Sides and inseams

  1. Lay front and back right sides together and sew both side seams.
  2. Sew the inseam in one run from hem to hem and reinforce the crotch junction.

4-3. Attach the cuffs

  1. Sew each cuff into a loop and fold it in half lengthwise.
  2. Pin it to the leg opening in quarters and sew, stretching the ribbing to fit.
  3. Cut the ribbing to about 85% of the leg opening — that difference is what makes the cuff hug the ankle.

4-4. Waist

  1. Fold the waist edge over twice to make a casing and stitch, leaving a gap.
  2. Thread the elastic, fit it and close the gap. Add eyelets and a cord at the front if you want a drawstring.

✏️ Ribbing must be shorter than the opening it goes on, or it does nothing. About 85% is the standard ratio: enough to pull in, not so much that it grips.

5. Tips & variations

  • Make a set: the same fabric as the hoodie gives you a matching tracksuit.
  • Add pockets: side-seam pockets are worth the ten extra minutes on trousers you will live in.
  • No ribbing: a plain double-fold hem gives straight-leg sweatpants instead of joggers.
