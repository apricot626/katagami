How to make a foldable shopping bag

A shopping bag that folds down into a small pocket sewn to its own side. In lightweight nylon it packs to the size of a purse, which is the only reason a spare bag ever actually gets carried.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (standard size)

  • Main fabric (lightweight nylon or fine cotton) — about 110 cm wide × 90 cm (44 × 35 in)
  • Fabric for the fold-away pocket — about 15 × 20 cm (6 × 8 in)
  • Optional: a snap or short elastic to hold it folded

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the body (base on the fold), the handles and the pocket.
  2. Cut
    Cut one body, two handles and one pocket.
  3. Finish the edges
    Serge or zigzag, or use French seams — lightweight nylon frays quickly.

4. Sewing

4-1. Make the handles and pocket

  1. Fold each handle in quarters lengthwise and topstitch both long edges.
  2. Turn the pocket's opening edge under twice and stitch, then press the other three edges under.

4-2. Attach the pocket

  1. Topstitch the pocket to the outside of the body, low on one side, while the fabric is still flat.
  2. This is the pocket the whole bag folds into, so stitch all three edges firmly.

4-3. Sew the body

  1. Fold the body at the base, right sides together, and sew both sides.
  2. Pinch each base corner into a triangle and sew across to form the gusset.

4-4. Finish the opening and handles

  1. Turn the opening edge under twice and stitch.
  2. Topstitch the handles to the inside of the opening with a stitched rectangle and a cross at each end.
  3. Lightweight fabric plus a full shopping bag is a lot of load on four small squares of stitching, so go over them twice.

✏️ Reinforce the handle attachments harder than feels necessary. Thin nylon tears at the stitch line long before the fabric itself gives way, and a bag of groceries finds that point immediately.

5. Tips & variations

  • French seams: they enclose every raw edge, which makes a thin bag far more durable.
  • Fold it small: roll from the base and tuck it into its pocket. A snap keeps it closed.
  • Ripstop: ripstop nylon is light, strong and packs down to almost nothing.
