How to make a scarf

You do not need to knit to make a scarf. Fold a rectangle lengthwise, sew, turn. Fleece or wool for winter, double gauze for spring. Far faster than knitting — which is exactly what you want when a gift deadline is closing in.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (standard size)

  • Main fabric (fleece, wool or double gauze) — about 110 cm wide × 60 cm (44 × 24 in)
  • Optional: a second fabric for a double-sided scarf

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Mark the measurements
    It is a rectangle, so mark it straight onto the fabric.
  2. Straighten the grain
    On something this long, a skewed grain shows. Pull a single thread and cut along the line it leaves.
  3. Cut
    Cut one piece for a folded scarf, or one of each fabric for a double-sided one.

4. Sewing

4-1. Sew the long edge

  1. Fold the piece lengthwise, right sides together, and sew the long edge with a 1 cm (⅜ in) allowance.
  2. For a double-sided version, place the two fabrics right sides together and sew both long edges.
  3. It is a long seam — support the fabric with your left hand so its weight does not drag it off line.

4-2. Sew one end and turn

  1. Sew one short end closed and leave the other open.
  2. Turn through the open end. A chopstick pushed inside speeds this up considerably.
  3. Roll the seam to the edge with your fingers and press.

4-3. Close the open end

  1. Fold the remaining raw edges inward and close with ladder stitch, or topstitch 3 mm from the edge.
  2. If you topstitch, do the other end to match.

4-4. Fringe (optional)

  1. Instead of closing the ends, pull the crosswise threads out one at a time to make a fringe.
  2. Stitch a line across at the depth you want first — 5–8 cm (2–3 in) — or the fringe keeps unravelling.

✏️ With fleece, most of this is unnecessary. Fleece does not fray, so cut a rectangle, snip the ends into a fringe, and you are done in fifteen minutes. That is the fastest gift on this site.

5. Tips & variations

  • Length: roughly your own height gives you options for how to wrap it. About 120 cm (47 in) suits a child.
  • Two-sided: different fabrics front and back show a glimpse of contrast as it falls.
  • For small children: a neck warmer is safer than a long scarf around playground equipment.
