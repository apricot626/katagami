How to make cargo pants

Work pants with big patch pockets on the sides. Because the waist is elastic there is no fly and no buttonholes — you sew the legs, then stitch the pockets on. Chino or ripstop holds the pocket shape; anything too soft and the pockets collapse.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (men's M)

  • Main fabric (chino, twill or ripstop) — about 110 cm wide × 220 cm (43 × 87 in)
  • Flat elastic (2.5–3 cm / 1 in wide) — about 90 cm (35 in)
  • Optional: snap fasteners for the pocket flaps — 2 sets

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    Trace the leg pattern, the side pockets and the pocket flaps.
  2. Cut
    Cut two fronts and two backs, two pockets, and the flaps in outer and lining.
  3. Finish the edges
    Serge or zigzag the crotch, side and inseam allowances.

4. Sewing

4-1. Make the pockets

  1. Turn the top edge of each pocket under twice and stitch — that is the opening.
  2. Press the other three edges under by 1 cm (⅜ in), folding the corners diagonally so the layers do not build up.
  3. Sew each flap in outer and lining, leaving the top edge open, then turn and topstitch.

4-2. Attach the pockets

  1. Position a pocket on each leg towards the side, 5–8 cm (2–3 in) below the rise so it does not dig in when you sit.
  2. Topstitch the three folded edges down 2–3 mm from the edge.
  3. Reinforce both top corners of the opening with a triangle or a square of stitching — that is where a patch pocket always tears first.
  4. Lay a flap above each pocket and stitch straight across the top.
  5. Do all of this before the legs become tubes — once the seams are sewn the presser foot cannot reach.

4-3. Sew the crotch seams

  1. Sew the two fronts together along the center front curve, then the two backs.
  2. Clip the curves every 5 mm (¼ in).

4-4. Sides and inseams

  1. Sew both side seams, then the inseam in one run from hem to hem. Reinforce the crotch junction.

4-5. Waist and hem

  1. Fold the waist edge over twice, stitch leaving a 4 cm (1½ in) gap, thread the elastic to about 85–90% of your waist, stitch the ends and close.
  2. Turn the hems up twice and stitch.

✏️ Attach the pockets while the legs are still flat pieces. Once you have sewn the side and inseam, the leg is a tube and the machine arm will not reach the pocket position.

5. Tips & variations

  • Measure what goes in: if the pocket is for a phone or wallet, measure it before you set the pocket size.
  • Add pocket gussets: folding a pleat into the sides and base gives depth for bulky things. Fiddly, but a real difference.
  • Shorts version: the shorts preset gives cargo shorts from the same pattern.
