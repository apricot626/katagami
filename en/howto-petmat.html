How to make a pet mat

A thin mat for a crate or a napping spot. Batting between two layers, with a grid of stitching so the filling stays put. Being thin, it washes and dries easily — which is why two of these beat one thick bed.

1. Materials & tools

Materials (medium dog)

  • Main fabric (cotton, oxford or boa) — about 110 cm wide × 80 cm (44 × 31 in)
  • Backing fabric (non-slip fabric works well) — the same amount
  • Medium batting — the same amount
  • Optional: bias tape if you bind the edges instead

Tools

  • Sewing machine (or needle and thread)
  • Fabric marker or pencil
  • Ruler and scissors
  • Iron
  • Pins or clips

2. Make the pattern (auto-generated in Katagami)

  1. Open the tool
    Open the pattern tool and choose “Pets” → “Pet mat” from the tabs at the top.
  2. Enter the size
    Enter the width, depth and corner radius. Presets cover cat/small dog, medium and large.
  3. Set the seam allowance
    Use the slider at the bottom (beginners: 1.0–1.5 cm / about 3/8–5/8 in).
  4. Print
    Press “Print (actual size)”, then in the print dialog set Scale = 100% and turn “Fit to page” OFF. Check the 50 mm calibration box on the first guide sheet with a ruler.

3. Cutting the fabric

  1. Trace the pattern
    With the corner radius at zero it is a rectangle, so mark it straight onto the fabric.
  2. Cut
    Cut one main, one backing and one batting, cutting the batting 1 cm smaller all round.
  3. Finish the edges
    Not needed — everything is enclosed.

4. Sewing

4-1. Layer the three pieces

  1. Stack backing right side up, main fabric right side down, batting on top.
  2. Clip all four sides. It is a large piece, so use plenty.

4-2. Sew and turn

  1. Sew all the way around, leaving a 20 cm (8 in) gap.
  2. Clip the corner curves and trim the corners.
  3. Turn through the gap. It is bulky with the batting in, so ease it through gradually.

4-3. Topstitch

  1. Fold the gap allowance in and press.
  2. Topstitch 1 cm (⅜ in) from the edge all the way around, closing the gap.

4-4. Quilt a grid

  1. Mark a grid at 10–15 cm (4–6 in) intervals.
  2. Stitch along the lines, always working from the center outward so the batting cannot bunch ahead of the needle.

✏️ Do not skip the grid. Batting caught only at the edges migrates to one side in the first wash and never comes back. And always quilt from the center outward — starting at an edge leaves you with a ripple at the far end.

5. Tips & variations

  • Non-slip backing: sold in craft shops. It stops the mat sliding on a wooden floor.
  • Make two: pet things get dirty. One in use, one in the wash.
  • Bind instead: layer the three wrong sides together and bind the edge, and you skip the turning entirely — much easier at large sizes.
